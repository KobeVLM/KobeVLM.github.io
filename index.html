🛡️ Business Continuity & Disaster Recovery Planning

One-day cram guide: notes → flashcards → quiz. Based on your lecture slides.

1. Basics

Disaster = any natural or man-made event that disrupts business operations so significantly that a considerable, coordinated effort is needed to recover.

NaturalMan-made
Geological: earthquake, volcano, tsunami, landslide, sinkhole
Meteorological: hurricane, tornado, wind/hail/ice/snow/rain storms, lightning
Other: avalanche, fire, flood, meteors, solar storms
Health: widespread illness, quarantine, pandemic		 Labor: strikes, walkouts, slow-downs
Social-political: war, terrorism, sabotage, vandalism, civil unrest, protests, cyber attacks, blockades
Materials: fires, hazmat spills
Utilities: power/comm outages, water or fuel shortages, radioactive fallout

How disasters hurt businesses

  • Direct damage to facilities & equipment
  • Transportation damage → delays deliveries, supplies, customers, employees
  • Communications outages
  • Utilities outages

2. BCP vs DRP

Both support the Availability pillar of C-I-A (Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability).

BCPDRP
FocusKeep critical business processes goingAssess, salvage, repair, restore damaged facilities/systems
UsesAlternate personnel, equipment, facilitiesOften IT systems focused
ScopeOften includes non-IT aspectsRecovery of the original site/systems
Prevention here ≠ preventing the disaster. It means preventing surprise & disorganized response, reducing impact (equipment bracing, fire detection/suppression, contingency plans) and avoiding long downtime.

Why needed (Gartner 2001)

Customers expect continued/rapid-resume service · Shareholders expect management control · Employees expect livelihoods protected (suppliers too) · Regulators expect requirements met regardless.

Benefits

Reduced risk · Process improvements · Organizational maturity · Availability & reliability · Marketplace advantage

4. Running the Project

Main phases: Pre-project → BIA → Develop plans → Test plans. Then maintain, test, audit regularly.

Pre-project activities

  • Obtain executive support
  • Formally define scope
  • Choose project team
  • Develop project plan / get a project manager
  • Write the project charter = document listing all of these plus budget & milestones (projected costs + measurable goals)

5. Business Impact Assessment (BIA)

  1. Survey critical processes (interview a rep per department, find dependencies on systems/people/equipment, collate into a database/spreadsheet)
  2. Risk & threat analysis – threats, vulnerabilities, risks per process; rank by probability, impact, cost; identify mitigating controls
  3. Determine MTD
  4. Establish recovery targets (RTO, RPO)

Risk = probability of a material deviation from an anticipated outcome (up or down; exists only if there's an objective).

3 areas to analyze: Threats (probabilities) · Assets (physical, financial, lost revenue, recovery costs, fines, goodwill) · Mitigating factors (UPS, generators, fire control, card readers)

The four key terms

TermMeaningMemory hook
MTD Maximum Tolerable DowntimeLongest a process can be down before significant damage / long-term viability threatened"Point of no return"
RTO Recovery Time ObjectiveTime from disaster onset to resumption of the process (must be ≤ MTD)T = Time to be back up
RPO Recovery Point ObjectiveMax data loss, counting backwards from onset; work that must be redoneP = Point in time of last good data
Criticality analysisRank processes by MTD, RTO, RPO, cost of downtime, reputation/market share/goodwill"Who gets fixed first"
Order: RTO ≤ MTD. Example: MTD 2 days → RTO must be shorter.

Other BIA items

  • Statements of impact – e.g., can't process payments, produce invoices, access customer data
  • Key metrics – cost to operate, cost of downtime, profit from process
  • Current capabilities – documented continuity/recovery, undocumented ones, "what if it happened tomorrow?"
  • Get senior management buy-off on MTD, RTO, RPO
  • Improve resilience for the most critical processes: biggest risks, cost of mitigation, combine controls, follow best practice

Sample recovery objectives (RTO) and technology

Recovery timeTechnology
8–14 daysNew equipment, data recovery from backup
4–7 daysCold systems, recovery from backup
2–3 daysWarm systems, recovery from backup
12–24 hrsWarm systems, high-speed backup media
6–12 hrsHot systems, high-speed backup media
3–6 hrsHot systems, data replication
1–3 hrsClustering, data replication
< 1 hrClustering, near real-time replication

Shorter target = more expensive tech. (The slide labels this column "RPO" but the values are recovery times.)

6. Developing the Plans

Recovery team selection criteria

Residence location vs. work · Skills & experience · Ability/willingness to respond · Health & family · Identify backups (other members, external resources)

Emergency response

  • Personnel safety (first aid, searching for people) – #1 concern always
  • Evacuation procedures
  • Asset protection (buildings, vehicles, equipment)
  • Damage assessment (may need outside structural engineers)
  • Emergency notification (response team, management, staff)

Damage assessment & salvage

Inside experts + usually outside experts (civil engineers, government building inspectors). Salvage = identify working/salvageable assets, cannibalize for parts.

Notification

Employees, suppliers, customers, regulators, authorities, shareholders, community. Methods: phone call trees, website, signage, media. Identify alternate means.

Personnel safety

Evacuation, accounting for everyone, first aid, emergency supplies (water, food, blankets, shelter). Staff may be stranded for days.

Communications

  • Avoid common infrastructure (not the same wires as normal comms)
  • Diversify mobile providers
  • Two-way radios · Satellite phones · Amateur radio

Utilities & infrastructure

Electricity (UPS, generator) · Water (no water = no fire suppression → building may close) · Natural gas (heating) · Wastewater (if disabled, building may close) · Steam heat

Logistics & supplies

Food/water, blankets/cots, sanitation, tools, spare parts, waste bins, information, communications, fire protection (extinguishers, sprinklers, smoke/fire alarms)

Business resumption planning

Alternate work locations · Alternate personnel · Communications · Standby assets/equipment · Access to procedures & business records

Restoration & recovery

Repair facilities/equipment · Replacement equipment · Restore utilities · Resume operations in the primary facility

Improving system resilience

  • Off-site media storage → assures data recovery
  • Server clusters → availability (geographic clusters = members far apart)
  • Data replication → current data on multiple servers, even remote (application, DBMS, OS, or hardware level)

Training staff

Everyday operations · Recovery procedures · Emergency procedures · Resumption procedures

7. Testing – 5 Levels (low → high risk)

#TestWhoKey idea
1Document reviewIndividualsRead plans & procedures, give feedback to owners
2WalkthroughTeamsGroup discussion of plans; brainstorming finds new issues. No fictional emergency
3SimulationTeamsWalkthrough using a scripted scenario / case study; mental disaster setting
4Parallel testTeamsFull/partial workload on recovery systems; production keeps running
5Cutover testTeamsProduction shut down; recovery systems take full load. Risk of interrupting real business, but most confidence
Mnemonic: Don't Worry, Simulate, Prepare, Cutover (Document, Walkthrough, Simulation, Parallel, Cutover)

8. Maintaining Plans

Review & modify when there are changes in: business processes/procedures · IT systems & applications · IT architecture · additions to IT apps · service providers · organizational structure.

9. Executing / Activating the DRP

Flow: Event occurs → Assess situation → (Not a disaster → stand down) / (Classed as disaster → Contact recovery team → Execute plan → Re-assess → continue recovery or stand down)

LevelAction
🟢 GreenNo action; advise DRP manager
🟠 AmberAdvise DRP manager; implement appropriate aspects of DRP

Most likely exam traps

  • RTO vs RPO: Time to be back up vs. amount of data you can lose
  • MTD is the business limit; RTO is the target and must fit inside it
  • BCP = keep running (alternate site/people) · DRP = repair & restore (often IT)
  • Walkthrough has no scenario; Simulation has a scenario
  • Parallel keeps production running; Cutover shuts it down
  • Personnel safety is always #1
  • HIPAA → healthcare / pharmacy
  • Project charter contains budget + measurable milestones
